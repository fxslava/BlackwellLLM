### Why the two gains do not compose: the boundary diagnostic

A *uniform* source is exactly what a companding warp produces. At equal codepoint count (2^32 either lattice in the [-8,+7] box):

| Uniform source | Z^8 MSE/coord | E8 MSE/coord | E8 gain | coords on box edge |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| filling the box | 0.083331 | 0.075916 | **+0.405 dB** | 12.5% |
| held 4 units inside the box | 0.083336 | 0.071678 | **+0.654 dB** | 0.0% |

Away from the faces the lattice delivers +0.654 dB, matching the asymptotic G_8 prediction of +0.654 dB, which validates the quantizer. Filling the box costs 0.250 dB: Z^8's unit cells tile a box exactly and overload nowhere, while 66% of E8's cells are clipped by a face. Companding is precisely the operation that flattens the source onto that box, so it buys density matching and spends part of the packing gain. The two are in tension, not additive, and **+0.405 dB is therefore the hard ceiling** on what a box-constrained companded E8 can win over NF4 at this coordinate width -- the asymptotic +0.65 dB is not reachable here at all.

The tension is visible in the fitted quantizers, not just in theory: the per-row MSE search lands on steps that leave only ~0.5% of coordinates on the box edge rather than the 12.5% a fully companded source would produce. It is declining full companding in order to stay away from the faces, which is why the measured margin comes in near half the ceiling.
### Silicon / register audit (static instruction count, not a measured kernel)

| Format | loads /8w | int ALU /8w | table entries | lookups /8w | sel ops per lookup | ops /weight | table bytes | ALU as % of memory time |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1D-NF4 / Lloyd-Max (16-LUT) | 1 | 16 | 16 | 8 | 4 | 7.0 | 64 | 26.2% |
| 8D-E8 uniform | 1 | 25 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 5.1 | 0 | 19.2% |
| 8D-E8 radial shell (C1) | 1 | 35 | 16 | 1 | 4 | 6.9 | 64 | 25.8% |
| 8D-E8 Cartesian 32-LUT (C2) | 1 | 25 | 32 | 8 | 5 | 9.1 | 128 | 34.2% |

At 4.013 bpw one tensor is 28.1 MB; reading it at 564 GB/s takes 50 us. every format reads identical bytes, so at batch=1 the GEMV is bandwidth-bound and all of these dequant costs stay under the memory time; the discriminator is not op count but whether a per-lane table access is needed.

Notes:

- **1D-NF4 / Lloyd-Max (16-LUT)** -- 8 per-lane lookups; 4-level FSEL tree or a PRMT pair; the only format needing a per-weight table access
- **8D-E8 uniform** -- 7x(SHF+LOP3), high-3 extract, parity via POPC(word & 0x11111110); no table
- **8D-E8 radial shell (C1)** -- adds 8 IMAD for R^2 and a bucketize; ONE table lookup per 8 weights, so the table cost amortizes 8x against the 1-D schemes
- **8D-E8 Cartesian 32-LUT (C2)** -- 8 per-lane lookups into 32 entries (5-level tree); replaces I2F+scale with the table, so it is close to NF4's cost plus E8's unpack

**Ascend AIV:** NOT QUANTIFIED. The AIV's vector ISA, its gather/table primitives and their issue costs are outside what can be verified from here, and inventing instruction counts for it would be worse than leaving the column empty. What carries over architecturally, and is all that is claimed: uniform E8 and radial-shell E8 need no per-element table, so they map onto any vector unit with bit-extract + integer-to-float + FMA; NF4/Lloyd-Max and Cartesian-LUT E8 need a per-element lookup into 16 or 32 entries, which on a vector machine without a cheap in-register permute becomes either a gather or a select chain of depth log2(entries). Candidate 1's one-lookup-per-8-weights is the only structural difference that would survive a port.

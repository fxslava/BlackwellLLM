# E8 Lattice VQ + QJL — empirical evaluation on GLM-4-9B activations

Generated by `research/run_full_engine_eval.py`; raw numbers in `research/bench_report.json`.
Target: `model.layers.10.mlp.down_proj.weight`, `[4096, 13696]`, 56,098,816 params, bf16.
Activations: 512 wikitext-2 tokens (4 × 128) through the real layer-0..10 prefix.
Host: RTX 5070 (11.9 GB), torch 2.13.0.dev+cu130, `nvidia.nvcomp` 5.3.0.

## 0. The prior analysis reproduces

An independent reimplementation hits all three published reference rows, which is what
licenses trusting the new columns:

| Scheme | Claimed SQNR | Measured | Δ | Claimed L∞ | Measured |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| RTN INT4 | 14.36 dB | **14.361 dB** | +0.001 | 0.0508 | **0.05078** |
| Lloyd-Max 16-LUT | 19.65 dB | **19.580 dB** | −0.070 | 0.5776 | **0.57243** |
| Pure E8 @3.5 bpw | 20.203 dB | **20.168 dB** | −0.035 | 0.0137 | **0.01384** |

Reproducing E8 at *exactly* 3.500 bpw also pinned down the rate definition the prior
work used, which was not stated and turns out to matter more than any other single
choice in this study (§2): it is the per-coordinate entropy **minus one bit per block**,
the credit for D8's even-sum constraint making the 8th coordinate's parity redundant.
The E8 decoder itself is verified exact by brute-force search against 4000 competing
lattice points per vector (`e8_lattice_engine.py --self-test`).

One correction to the premise: the weights are *not* quasi-Gaussian in the tails.
Kurtosis is 4.25 and `max|w| / rms = 52.9`. One outlier per row is what collapses
max-scaled RTN INT4 to 14.4 dB, and it is why the fair-comparison rows below exist.

## 1. E8 keeps its >20 dB advantage on real activations — but weight SQNR mis-ranks schemes

| Scheme | Total bpw | Fixed-width bpw | W-SQNR | **Act-SNR** | Mean bias / out-rms | bias *t* | W-L∞ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| BF16 baseline | 16.000 | 16.000 | ∞ | ∞ | 0 | — | 0 |
| RTN INT4 | 4.001 | 4.000 | 14.36 | 14.84 | +8.2e−5 | +0.7 | 0.0508 |
| RTN INT4 (MSE clip) | 4.001 | 4.000 | 18.43 | 17.57 | +1.2e−3 | **+12.0** | 0.4610 |
| Lloyd-Max 16-LUT | 4.001 | 4.000 | 19.58 | 17.12 | +1.4e−4 | +1.5 | 0.5724 |
| **Pure E8 @3.5 bpw** | **3.502** | 8.125 | 20.17 | **20.65** | −2.1e−4 | −3.3 | 0.0138 |
| **Dense E8 @4.0 bpw** | **4.001** | 8.125 | 23.19 | **23.70** | +6.6e−5 | +1.7 | 0.0104 |
| E8 fixed-width 4b *(superseded, see Part II)* | 4.126 | 4.125 | 17.76 | 15.86 | +8.6e−4 | +8.0 | 0.5904 |
| Hybrid E8 + QJL | 4.003 | 8.626 | n/a | 15.88 | −2.1e−4 | −2.2 | n/a |

E8 at 3.5 bpw does hold up: 20.65 dB on real activations, *better* than its 20.17 dB
weight SQNR, and 21.82 dB on the layer-3 replication.

The more useful finding is that **weight SQNR does not rank schemes the way Act-SNR
does.** Lloyd-Max has the better weight SQNR of the two 4-bit scalar schemes (19.58 vs
18.43) but the worse Act-SNR (17.12 vs 17.57). The layer-3 replication makes the point
violently: Lloyd-Max holds 19.71 dB weight SQNR there and delivers **4.51 dB** Act-SNR.
Layer 3's activations have kurtosis 2.5e5 with `|x|` up to 7.19 against an rms of 0.0071,
and Lloyd-Max clips weight outliers hard (L∞ 0.57, 41× E8's). When a clipped weight sits
in a channel an outlier activation reads, the output error is unbounded.

So E8's practical merit is not its 0.6 dB SQNR margin — it is that a lattice quantizer
never clips. That is a property worth having independently of rate.

Bias is a non-finding, and in the direction opposite to the hypothesis. Pure E8's mean
output bias is −2.1e−4 of the output rms; there is no drift for a residual sketch to
remove. The only schemes with strongly significant bias (*t* = +12.0, +8.0) are the two
that clip, and clipping bias is a shrinkage a zero-mean sketch cannot correct either.

## 2. The headline rate is an entropy-coded rate, and that is the whole engineering story

The same E8-quantized tensor has five defensible bit rates. At the 3.5 bpw operating
point:

| Rate definition | bpw | Random access? |
|---|---|---|
| Per-coordinate entropy, parity-credited (the published figure) | **3.501** | no |
| Per-coordinate entropy, factorized | 3.626 | no |
| Joint block entropy | *saturates* — only 7.0 M blocks exist, ceiling 2.84 bpw | no |
| **Fixed-width coordinates + coset bit** | **8.125** | **yes** |
| Dense index into observed points | meaningless — LUT ≈ the tensor | yes |

The addressable rate is **2.3× the headline rate** *for the entropy-coded operating
point*. The stronger claim this section originally made — that a fixed-width E8 loses
outright to the 4-bit scalar schemes — **was wrong, and Part II retracts it.** It rested on
a quantizer that clamped coordinates after decoding and used a single global step; done
properly (box-constrained decode, per-row step, 32 bits per block) fixed-rate E8 reaches
18.68 dB Act-SNR at 4.001 bpw and beats every scalar baseline here.

The redeeming result is that the entropy rate is **reachable with a real GPU codec**.
Storing coordinates byte-aligned (8.125 bpw raw) and letting nvCOMP's ANS entropy-code
it gives:

| Layout | Codec | Effective bpw | Decomp | Throughput |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| E8 @3.5, bit-packed | ANS | 6.646 | 0.374 ms | 153 GB/s |
| **E8 @3.5, byte-aligned** | **ANS** | **3.761** | **0.333 ms** | **171 GB/s** |
| E8 @4.0, byte-aligned | ANS | 4.259 | 0.319 ms | 179 GB/s |

3.761 bpw against the 3.626 bpw factorized-entropy ideal is 96% coding efficiency; the
missing 0.135 bpw is almost exactly the 0.125 bpw parity credit, which ANS cannot see
because it does not know about the even-sum constraint. Bit-packing *hurts* — it straddles
byte lanes and hides the fields from the compressor (6.65 vs 3.76 bpw).

So at *this* operating point E8 is a transfer format: ~3.76 bpw on NVMe/PCIe, decompressed
to an 8.125 bpw VRAM-resident buffer a GEMV kernel can address, buying transfer bandwidth
and disk footprint but not VRAM. Part II shows that is a statement about the
entropy-coded configuration, not about the lattice: a purpose-built fixed-rate E8 is a
perfectly good kernel format at 4.00 bpw.

*(Measurement note: the first version of this harness byte-padded each 65-bit block to 9
bytes. The padding bits are constant, and they inflated every compression ratio — Pure E8
under GDeflate read 1.89 instead of 1.26. The packing is now bit-exact.)*

## 3. QJL at 0.5 bpw cannot work, and the premise's own isotropy result is why

For a projection row `s ~ N(0, I_D)`, `E[⟨s,x⟩ sign(⟨s,e⟩)] = √(2/π)·⟨x, e/‖e‖⟩`, so
`⟨x,e⟩ ≈ √(π/2)·(‖e‖/m)·⟨Sx, sign(Se)⟩` is unbiased. Its standard deviation is
`‖x‖‖e‖√(π/2m)`, while the inner product it estimates has rms `‖x‖‖e‖/√D` for a residual
with no preferred direction. The ratio of noise added to signal removed is

    noise / signal = √(π·D / 2m)   →   √π = 1.77 at m = D/2, independent of D

Isotropy — the premise's own conclusion #1 — is exactly what forces `⟨x,e⟩` to be small
and self-cancelling, and therefore cheaper to ignore than to sketch at 1 bit.

Measurement agrees to within 0.2 dB across a 16× range of m:

| m | bpw | Act-SNR (Rademacher) | Δ measured | Δ predicted |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8 | 0.125 | 9.87 | −10.79 | −10.99 |
| 16 | 0.250 | 12.77 | −7.88 | −7.98 |
| **32** | **0.500** | **15.88** | **−4.77** | **−4.97** |
| 64 | 1.000 | 18.56 | −2.10 | −1.96 |
| 96 | 1.500 | 20.37 | −0.28 | −0.20 |
| 128 | 2.000 | 21.69 | +1.04 | +1.05 |

Break-even is `m = πD/2 = 1.571 bpw`, three times the spec's budget. Gaussian
projections match Rademacher. Structured **FWHT projections do better** — their rows are
orthogonal, so the measurements are non-redundant — reaching break-even at m = 64
(1.0 bpw, +2.50 dB); but they saturate at m = D, since only D orthogonal rows exist, and
asking for more actively hurts by double-weighting repeated rows.

**The head-to-head the study was for**, at matched total rate:

| Use of the marginal 0.5 bpw | Total bpw | Act-SNR |
|---|---|---|
| E8 3.5 bpw + QJL m=32 | 4.003 | **15.88 dB** |
| E8 grid density, 3.5 → 4.0 bpw | 4.001 | **23.70 dB** |

**Lattice density wins by 7.8 dB.** Confirmed on layer 3 (16.90 dB hybrid vs 21.82 dB
pure E8 at 0.5 bpw less). QJL is the wrong tool here; it is designed for KV-cache keys,
where the sketch replaces a vector that would otherwise be absent entirely, not for
correcting a residual whose inner products are already near zero.

Two secondary results worth keeping: per-block residual norms are unnecessary (their
relative spread inside a row is 3.9%, and row-norms give an identical 15.878 vs 15.880 dB),
so the spec's 0.5 bpw accounting *is* achievable rather than secretly 0.75 bpw — it just
does not help. And the spec's literal `(X Sᵀ) ⊙ B` is dimensionally inconsistent
([tokens, m] against [out, m] with no contraction named); the implemented estimator is
the standard QJL one above.

## 4. The RTX 5070 cannot hide whole-layer decompression behind the compute window

Per-tensor, the answer looks positive: ANS decompresses `down_proj` in 0.333 ms, inside
the 0.46 ms budget. Two corrections remove that conclusion.

**`down_proj` is 27% of a layer.** A GLM-4 layer is 206 M params. Scaling the measured
rate up: **1.20 ms per layer, 2.6× over the 0.46 ms budget.** Only Bitcomp and ANS are
even in the running (0.32–0.38 ms/tensor); GDeflate, LZ4 and Zstd run 1.7–4.6 ms, 5–25×
too slow.

**The budget is smaller than 0.46 ms once you quantize.** Measured peak bandwidth on this
card is **564 GB/s**, not the 900 GB/s assumed — the premise's figure belongs to a larger
Blackwell part. A layer's own weight read is therefore 0.731 ms at bf16, but only
**0.172 ms at 3.761 bpw**: quantizing shrinks the very window the prefetch has to hide in.
Against the real quantized window, decompression is ~7× over.

This is not fatal, but it constrains the design:

- **Prefetch depth ≥ 3–4 layers**, not 1. At 2.6× over a single layer's budget, three
  layers of lead time covers it — at the cost of holding that many decompressed layers
  (~209 MB each at 8.125 bpw) resident, which is a large fraction of a 12 GB card and
  competes with the KV cache the tiered-paging work is trying to keep there.
- **Decompression is not free concurrency.** ANS runs on the same SMs and the same DRAM
  as the GEMV; the 171 GB/s and the 564 GB/s are drawn from one budget, so overlap is
  contention, not parallelism.
- Where compression unambiguously pays is the path it was measured for in
  `docs/TIERED_KV_AND_AOT.md`: NVMe/PCIe at ~7 GB/s is 25× slower than ANS decode, so on
  a cold start or a hibernation wake, 3.76 bpw at 171 GB/s is pure win.

## 5. Scope — what this does and does not establish

This is a PyTorch-side algorithmic study on a research branch. It does **not** touch
`blackwell_kernels` or `blackwell_core`: Act-SNR is measured with cuBLAS fp32 GEMMs, not
with the engine's batch-1 GEMV, and no E8 dequant kernel exists. "On the engine" here
means *with the engine's real weights, real activations, real GPU and real codec*, not
through its kernels.

Other limits, stated plainly: 512 calibration tokens; one tensor type (`down_proj`) in 2
of 40 layers; one checkpoint; no end-to-end perplexity or task metric, so Act-SNR is a
per-layer proxy whose mapping to output quality is not established here — §1 shows that
even the *weight*→*activation* step reorders schemes, so activation→quality deserves the
same suspicion. Rates above 4.5 bpw and the interaction with incoherence processing
(random rotations, as in QuIP#), which is the standard fix for exactly the outlier problem
that sinks fixed-width E8, were not tested.

## Recommendation

1. **Drop QJL** for weight residuals. The 7.8 dB deficit against plain lattice density
   follows from a closed form that measurement confirms across 16× in m; there is no
   configuration of it at ≤1 bpw that wins.
2. **Adopt E8 as a transfer/storage codec, not a weight format** — byte-aligned
   coordinates + nvCOMP ANS, 3.76 bpw effective at 171 GB/s, decompressing to an 8.125 bpw
   addressable buffer. Wire it into the DirectStorage/hibernation path, where the 7 GB/s
   NVMe bottleneck makes it unambiguously profitable.
3. **Do not plan on hiding decompression behind one layer** on this card. Budget 3–4
   layers of prefetch lead, and account for the resident decompressed working set against
   the KV cache.
4. **If the goal is a 4 bpw VRAM-resident format**, see §16: NF4 at exactly 4.000 bpw plus
   0.1% sparse outlier retention, with a per-row MSE-optimal scale. *(This item pointed at
   Lloyd-Max in Part I and at fixed-rate E8 in Part II; §12 and §14 retract both.)*
5. **Score candidates on Act-SNR, never weight SQNR alone**, and always on more than one
   layer. Layer 3 turns a 19.71 dB weight SQNR into 4.51 dB of output SNR; a weight-only
   screen would have shipped it.

# Part II — Fixed-rate 4.00 bpw E8 with outlier isolation

Generated by `research/eval_fixed_rate_e8.py`; tables in
[FIXED_RATE_E8.md](FIXED_RATE_E8.md), raw numbers in `fixed_rate_e8_report.json`.
No entropy coder and no nvCOMP anywhere in this part: 8 coordinates in one 32-bit
register, closed-form decode.

## 6. The format: 32 bits per block, and the parity trick that pays for the coset flag

Eight coordinates in [-8, +7] would consume all 32 bits, leaving nothing for the coset
flag. D8's even-sum constraint buys it back — with `Σkᵢ ≡ 0 (mod 2)`, the low bit of `k₇`
is implied by the other seven, so only its high 3 bits are stored:

    bit  0     coset flag        bits 1–28  k₀..k₆ (offset binary, 4b each)
    bits 29–31 high 3 bits of k₇   decode   k₇ = (high3 << 1) | (Σᵢ<₇ uᵢ & 1)

It generalizes: `1 + 7b + (b−1) = 8b` bits, i.e. **exactly `b` bpw at any coordinate
width**, coset carried free. b=4 is the 32-bit register case; b=5 is used in §9.

**This is why coordinates cannot be clamped after decoding**, which is what the Part I
quantizer did. Clamping flips the parity of the sum, the even-sum invariant breaks, and the
decoder then silently reconstructs the wrong `k₇` on exactly the saturating blocks. The
quantizer here is box-constrained *by construction* — round into the box, then repair
parity with the cheapest single ±1 move that stays inside it — and the pack is verified to
round-trip bit-exactly over the real tensor on every run.

## 7. Retraction: done properly, fixed-rate E8 beats every scalar baseline at 4.00 bpw

> **Superseded by §12.** The comparison below is against scalar baselines that did *not* get
> the per-row MSE-optimal scale search E8 was given. With that search the scalar schemes gain
> ~3.4 dB and E8 loses by 0.6–0.9 dB. The ablation of E8 against its own earlier self
> (below) still stands; the cross-scheme verdict does not.

| Layer 10, 4.001 bpw | W-SQNR | Act-SNR | CosSim |
|---|---|---|---|
| RTN INT4 | 14.36 | 14.84 | 0.983876 |
| Lloyd-Max 16-LUT | 19.58 | 17.12 | 0.990314 |
| **E8 4.00 bpw (direct)** | 19.49 | **18.68** | **0.993230** |

Part I reported 15.86 dB Act-SNR at 4.126 bpw and concluded the lattice was the wrong
candidate for a kernel format. That was an artifact of the quantizer, not the lattice.
Ablated:

| | rate | W-SQNR | Act-SNR |
|---|---|---|---|
| post-clamp `[-7,7]`, one global step *(Part I)* | 4.126 | 17.76 | 15.86 |
| boxed decode, `[-8,7]`, 32-bit pack, global step | 4.001 | 19.33 | 16.92 |
| + **per-row step** | 4.001 | **19.49** | **18.68** |

+1.06 dB from getting the format right, **+1.76 dB from per-row step selection alone** —
and that one is free, because the per-row FP16 scale is stored either way. Note it barely
moves weight SQNR (+0.16 dB) while moving Act-SNR by 1.76 dB: minimizing MSE row by row
redistributes error away from the high-norm output rows that dominate the output, which a
single global step cannot do. **Per-row step search is the cheapest dB in this study.**

## 8. Sparse outlier retention replaces FWHT in the runtime path — but neither reaches 0.999

The direct answer to the brief: **no, sparse outlier retention does not hold CosSim >
0.999 at 4 bpw.** The best 4-bpw-class result is 0.997777 (layer 3, α=1.0%) and 0.995806
(layer 10). But it does answer the question behind it — *can we drop the FWHT?* — with a
yes:

| Layer 3 | Eff. bpw | Act-SNR | CosSim | runtime transform? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| E8 direct | 4.001 | 7.18 | 0.902904 | no |
| + FWHT (128-block) | 4.001 | 18.82 | 0.994258 | **yes** |
| + FWHT + 107-block mix | 4.001 | 21.59 | 0.996524 | **yes** |
| + sparse α=0.1% (13 channels) | 4.015 | 21.79 | 0.996733 | no |
| + sparse α=0.5% | 4.062 | 22.79 | 0.997392 | no |
| + sparse α=1.0% | 4.122 | 23.51 | 0.997777 | no |

**Retaining 13 of 13696 channels (+0.014 bpw) beats a full randomized Hadamard**, and beats
the block-mixed variant too. On layer 10 the two are a tie (α=0.5% → 20.45 dB vs FWHT
20.47 dB). Combining them adds nothing over the better one alone. Since the FWHT has to run
on X at every decode step and sparse retention is a static reindex of the weights, the
sparse path is the one to build.

Two caveats that matter. FWHT wins clearly on the tail metrics — min-token CosSim 0.9948 vs
0.9874 at α=0.1%, and L∞(Y) 0.758 vs 0.133 — because it spreads error uniformly instead of
leaving a few unprotected channels exposed. And the ranking is layer-dependent: on layer 3,
E8-direct collapses to 7.18 dB and plain **RTN INT4 (17.22 dB) beats it**, because
max-scaled RTN never clips. Not clipping is worth more than resolution on spiky layers.

**Coordinate saturation is a bad proxy for quality.** It sits at 0.59–0.72% across every
4-bpw variant and moves the wrong way: FWHT *raises* saturation (0.657% → 0.704% on
layer 3) while adding 11.6 dB. What matters is not how many coordinates clip but whether
the clipped ones sit in channels the activations amplify.

## 9. What CosSim > 0.999 actually costs

Cosine similarity here is not independent information: for an error uncorrelated with the
signal, `CosSim ≈ 1 − 10^(−SNR/10)/2`, which reproduces every measured value to six
decimals (layer 10 direct: SNR 18.684 dB → predicted 0.993230, measured 0.993230). So
**CosSim > 0.999 is exactly Act-SNR > 27.0 dB**, and the cosine column can be read as a
restatement of the SNR column.

At 5.00 bpw (same pack, b=5) the target is reachable — but only with outlier handling:

| | Eff. bpw | Act-SNR | CosSim |
|---|---|---|---|
| Layer 3, E8 5.00 bpw direct | 5.001 | 9.08 | 0.937767 |
| Layer 3, E8 5.00 bpw + sparse α=0.5% | 5.057 | **27.91** | **0.999198** ✓ |
| Layer 10, E8 5.00 bpw + sparse α=0.5% | 5.057 | 25.33 | 0.998537 |

The layer-3 pair is the sharpest result in either part: **a whole extra bit per weight buys
+1.9 dB without outlier handling; 0.5% sparse retention buys +18.8 dB.** On outlier-heavy
layers, rate is nearly worthless until the outliers are dealt with. Layer 10 still misses
0.999 at 5.06 bpw, so the threshold is not reachable layer-uniformly below ~5.5 bpw here.

## 10. Recommendation (Part II)

1. ~~**Adopt the 32-bit fixed-rate E8 pack as the kernel-resident weight format** — 4.001 bpw,
   one register per 8 weights, closed-form decode, no entropy coder, and it beats INT4 and
   Lloyd-Max on Act-SNR at equal rate.~~ **Withdrawn by §16:** the scalar baselines it beat
   were under-tuned, and against fitted ones E8 loses by 0.6–0.9 dB. Use NF4.
2. **Always select the step per output row.** Free, and worth +1.76 dB Act-SNR.
3. **Use sparse outlier retention, not FWHT**, at α = 0.1–0.5% (+0.014 to +0.061 bpw). It
   matches or beats the Hadamard while keeping the runtime path transform-free. Keep FWHT
   in reserve for tail-sensitive work, where it is genuinely better.
4. **Budget ~5.1 bpw if a 0.999 cosine floor is a hard requirement**, and expect it to hold
   per-layer only with outlier retention — at 4 bpw the realistic floor is ~0.995–0.998.
5. **Select outliers per layer, not once globally.** Layer 3 needs 13 channels to gain
   14.6 dB; layer 10 gains 1.2 dB from the same spend. The profile is not transferable.

## 11. Scope (Part II)

Same limits as §5, and two more. The 32-bit pack is verified lossless in PyTorch but no
CUDA kernel decodes it, so the claimed "1 register, closed-form" cost is a property of the
format, not a measured kernel latency — an E8 dequant kernel in `blackwell_kernels` is
still the missing piece. And sparse outlier retention makes the GEMV *irregular*: a bulk
E8 pass plus a small dense BF16 pass over gathered columns. At batch=1 that second pass is
13–137 columns × 4096 rows, which is cheap in FLOPs but is a separate kernel launch and a
gather; whether it is free in wall-clock is unmeasured here.

# Part III — 1-D scalar vs 8-D E8 at matched rate, scored to logit KL

Generated by `research/eval_1d_vs_e8_kl.py`; tables in [KL_1D_VS_E8.md](KL_1D_VS_E8.md),
raw numbers in `kl_comparison_report.json`. Every candidate carries the same 0.1% BF16
outlier channels (13 of 13696, chosen by ||X[:,k]||_2) and the same per-row MSE-optimal
scale search, so the only difference between rows is the codebook. Bit-identical across
repeat runs.

## 12. Retraction, again: E8's margin over an optimally-tuned 1-D scalar is *negative*

**Part II's claim that fixed-rate E8 beats every scalar baseline was an artifact of an
under-tuned baseline.** Part II gave E8 a per-row MSE-optimal step and gave Lloyd-Max only a
fixed rms normalization. Granting both the identical search lifts Lloyd-Max by ~3.4 dB on
layer 10 (17.12 → 20.50 dB) and reverses the ranking:

| Layer 10, 4.013 bpw, identical outliers | Act-SNR | vs uniform RTN |
|---|---|---|
| 1D-RTN-INT4 (uniform) | 18.745 | — |
| **1D-Lloyd-Max** | **20.495** | **+1.75** |
| 1D-NF4 | 20.376 | +1.63 |
| 8D-E8-Fixed | 19.859 | +1.11 |

**The answer to the brief's question — the geometric margin of E8 over optimal 1-D
Lloyd-Max under identical outlier conditions — is −0.636 dB on layer 10 and −0.876 dB on
layer 3**, and E8 costs +0.0015 bpw more (block padding). E8 loses, at a slightly higher
rate.

The decomposition says why, and it is not a subtle effect:

* E8 beats *uniform* RTN by +1.11 dB (layer 10). Of that, ~0.65 dB is the genuine E8
  granular/packing gain and ~0.56 dB is simply the code that RTN's symmetric −7..+7
  convention throws away (10·log10((16/15)^2) = 0.56 dB). Against an asymmetric −8..+7
  INT4, E8's margin would be roughly its textbook 0.65 dB and no more.
* Lloyd-Max beats uniform RTN by +1.75 dB, purely by *companding* — placing levels where
  the density is.

On a weight distribution with kurtosis 4.25 and max/rms = 52.9, **companding gain beats
lattice packing gain.** E8's coordinates are uniformly spaced inside its box, so it earns
the packing gain and forfeits the companding gain; a 1-D non-uniform quantizer does the
opposite and wins. Nothing about the lattice fixes this — it is what a box-constrained
uniform lattice *is*.

## 13. The KL metric is floor-limited, which the controls establish rather than assume

Two synthetic-noise controls perturb Y to a known Act-SNR, so the KL scale can be
calibrated instead of trusted:

| Layer 10 | Act-SNR | KL mean (nats) |
|---|---|---|
| method floor (fp32 restart vs the model's own bf16 path) | — | 1.869e−4 |
| Ctrl-noise-40dB | 40.00 | 1.852e−4 |
| Ctrl-noise-30dB | 30.00 | 2.166e−4 |
| the actual schemes | ~20 | 5.1e−4 … 6.1e−4 |

A 10 dB change in perturbation moves KL by **17%, not 10x**. Both controls sit *at* the
floor. So logit KL saturates for anything cleaner than roughly 30 dB, and at the ~20 dB the
schemes actually deliver they sit only 2.7–4.8x the floor. Consequence: the unpaired mean KL
column **cannot rank these schemes** — the standard errors overlap. Only the paired
per-token comparison can, which is why it is reported with both a *t*-statistic and a
rank-based sign test (per-token KL has a max ~60x its mean, so *t* alone is fragile).

This is also why the scale-search MSE runs in float64: in float32, near-tied grid points are
resolved differently by reduction noise and the layer-3 effect size moved 25% between runs.

## 14. KL and Act-SNR disagree, and the disagreement is the interesting part

| Layer 3 | Act-SNR | KL mean | KL SE | KL max | CosSim min-token | Top-1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1D-RTN-INT4 | 20.818 | 1.049e−3 | 8.2e−5 | 2.03e−2 | 0.983930 | 0.9746 |
| 1D-Lloyd-Max | **22.649** | 1.619e−3 | 2.6e−4 | 9.84e−2 | 0.991030 | 0.9824 |
| **1D-NF4** | 22.619 | **1.050e−3** | **7.7e−5** | **1.60e−2** | **0.992388** | **0.9863** |
| 8D-E8-Fixed | 21.773 | 1.241e−3 | 2.1e−4 | 9.13e−2 | 0.987324 | 0.9805 |

On layer 3 Lloyd-Max wins Act-SNR by 0.876 dB and **loses on logit KL**: E8 has the lower KL
on 61.3% of tokens (z = +5.13, sign test; paired diff +3.77e−4 ± 1.1e−4, t = +3.47). On
layer 10 the KL gap between them is not significant either way (45.9% of tokens, z = −1.86).
So **Act-SNR does not predict logit KL** — the third time in this study that a cheaper
metric has mis-ranked schemes (weight SQNR → Act-SNR in §1, Act-SNR → KL here).

The mechanism is the tail, not the mean. Lloyd-Max's per-token KL has max 9.84e−2 and a
standard error 3.4x NF4's, because MSE-optimal centroids spend resolution where the mass is
and leave the tail coarsely covered — rare large errors, which is exactly what a log-domain
divergence punishes and what a squared-error metric averages away.

**NF4 is the quiet winner, and it was not the expected one.** At statistically identical
Act-SNR to Lloyd-Max (22.619 vs 22.649), it has the lowest mean KL on layer 3, a 6x lower
KL max, the tightest KL spread, the best min-token cosine, and the best top-1 agreement on
*both* layers — while being exactly 4.000 bpw, the simplest possible 16-entry LUT. Making
the *codes* equiprobable rather than the *error* minimal turns out to be the right objective
when the metric is a divergence.

## 15. Decode complexity does not rescue the lattice

| Format | loads / 8w | int ALU / 8w | LUT reads / 8w | ops / weight | ALU time | % of memory time |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| RTN INT4 / any 4-bit uniform | 1 | 24 | 0 | 5.0 | 9.3 us | 18.7% |
| 16-entry LUT (Lloyd-Max, NF4) | 1 | 16 | 8 | 7.0 | 13.1 us | 26.2% |
| E8 32-bit coset pack | 1 | 25 | 0 | 5.1 | 9.6 us | 19.2% |

Static op counts, not measured kernel latency. All four formats read **identical bytes**
(28.1 MB per tensor; 50 us at the measured 564 GB/s), so at batch = 1 the GEMV is
bandwidth-bound and every one of these dequant costs is hidden. E8's only architectural
advantage is needing no table — no shared-memory bank conflicts — but a 16-entry LUT fits in
registers behind a ~4-op select tree or a PRMT trick, so the edge is small and does not
offset −0.6 to −0.9 dB.

## 16. Recommendation (Part III) — supersedes Part II

1. **Do not write the E8 kernel.** At matched rate against properly-tuned 1-D baselines it
   loses on Act-SNR, needs +0.0015 bpw, and its decode advantage is hidden by bandwidth.
   Part II's recommendation 1 is withdrawn.
2. **Write the NF4 kernel**: exactly 4.000 bpw, one 16-entry LUT, best tail behaviour
   (6x lower KL max than Lloyd-Max), best top-1 agreement on both layers. Keep the 0.1%
   sparse BF16 outlier retention from Part II — that part held up and is worth 14.6 dB on
   layer 3 for +0.014 bpw.
3. **Score on logit KL with its controls, never on Act-SNR alone**, and use paired
   per-token tests. An Act-SNR screen would have shipped Lloyd-Max, whose rare-token
   behaviour is the worst of the three non-uniform schemes.
4. **If the lattice is still attractive, test companding + E8** — warp the values before the
   lattice decode so it earns the ~1.7 dB companding gain *and* the 0.65 dB packing gain.
   That is the only configuration that could plausibly clear NF4. If it does not, the 8-D
   lattice is not worth a kernel in this engine.
5. **Fit every baseline as hard as the candidate.** Both retractions in this document
   (§7, §12) came from the same mistake in opposite directions. The per-row MSE-optimal
   scale search is worth more (~3.4 dB) than the entire E8-vs-scalar question being argued
   about (~0.7 dB).

## 17. Scope (Part III)

Adds to §5 and §11. The KL is real — the residual stream is reassembled as `h1 + Yhat` and
layers L+1..39, the final norm and lm_head are actually run, with the layer loop verified
bit-identical to `GlmModel.forward` — but it is measured on 512 tokens against a floor of
~2e−4 nats, so it resolves a factor-of-two difference between schemes and nothing finer.
Logits are formed in fp32 over a bf16 tail; the tail's own bf16 rounding is part of the
floor. Two layers of one checkpoint, one tensor type, no perplexity or task metric, and the
decode audit remains an op count rather than a kernel measurement.

# Part IV — Companded E8: the two gains are in tension, not additive

Generated by `research/eval_companded_e8.py`; tables in [COMPANDED_E8.md](COMPANDED_E8.md),
raw numbers in `companded_e8_report.json`. Same 0.1% BF16 outliers and same per-row
MSE-optimal step search on every row; the 32-bit register contract (4.000 bpw bulk) is held
and verified lossless on every fit.

## 18. Verdict against the acceptance criteria

| Criterion | Result |
|---|---|
| A candidate recovers +0.65 dB Act-SNR over 1-D NF4 | **Not met.** Best is **+0.342 dB** (companded, layer 3); layer 10 is **−0.201 dB**. |
| Paired logit-KL sign test vs NF4 with z > +3.0 on **both** layers | **Not met.** Layer 3 **z = +5.66** (62.5% of tokens); layer 10 z = +0.53. |

The hypothesis as stated — companding (+1.75 dB) and Zador packing (+0.65 dB) composing to
+2.40 dB over uniform INT4 — is **partially** borne out against uniform INT4
(**+2.14 dB** measured on layer 3, +1.43 dB on layer 10) but **fails** where it matters,
against NF4, because NF4 already banks the companding term more efficiently than a lattice
can in a bounded box. The reason is structural and now measured.

## 19. Candidate 1 (radial shell scaling) is worth nothing, as its mathematics implies

| | Act-SNR L3 | Act-SNR L10 | W-SQNR L3 | W-SQNR L10 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8D-E8-Uniform | 21.77 | 19.86 | 19.71 | 19.50 |
| 8D-E8-Radial-16 | 21.72 | 19.85 | 19.74 | 19.52 |
| 8D-E8-Radial-64 | 21.72 | 19.85 | 19.74 | 19.53 |

Radial shell scaling leaves the encoder a nearest-neighbour search on the *uniform* lattice
and only moves reconstruction points. That is Lloyd's centroid condition, which cannot touch
the density term in Zador's expression, so it cannot earn companding gain — and it doesn't:
**+0.03 dB of weight SQNR, and −0.05 dB of Act-SNR.** 64 bins behave exactly like 16, so the
brief's concern about shell-LUT size is not the binding constraint. (The brief also states
there are "fewer than 16 unique active shells" in the 4-bit box; there are 72, R² is always
an even integer, and the median is R² = 30 — so a LUT indexed by R²/2 would saturate at the
median. The implementation bins by quantile instead. It made no difference.)

Top-1 agreement on layer 3 actually *drops* from 0.9805 to 0.9746. Candidate 1 is a reject
on every metric.

## 20. Candidate 2 works, and the ceiling is +0.405 dB — not +0.65 dB

Companding E8 is a real improvement over uniform E8: **+1.19 dB (layer 3), +0.32 dB
(layer 10)** Act-SNR, and +0.42 dB of weight SQNR on both. It is the only variant in four
parts of this study that ever beat NF4.

But the asymptotic +0.65 dB is unreachable in a bounded fixed-rate code, and the diagnostic
measures the real ceiling directly. On a *uniform* source — which is exactly what a
companding warp produces — at equal codepoint count (2³² either lattice in the [−8,+7] box):

| Uniform source | Z⁸ MSE/coord | E8 MSE/coord | E8 gain |
|---|---|---|---|
| held 4 units inside the box | 0.083336 | 0.071678 | **+0.654 dB** |
| filling the box | 0.083331 | 0.075916 | **+0.405 dB** |

Away from the faces the lattice delivers +0.654 dB, matching G₈'s prediction to three
decimals — which validates the quantizer and confirms the packing gain is real. Filling the
box costs **0.250 dB**, because **Z⁸'s unit cells tile a box exactly and overload nowhere,
while 66% of E8's cells are clipped by a face.** A cubic lattice is the one lattice that
perfectly tiles the code box, and that is a structural advantage no amount of shaping removes.

So companding *buys* density matching and *spends* part of the packing gain. **+0.405 dB is
the hard ceiling** on companded E8 over NF4 at 4 bits per coordinate; the +0.65 dB in the
acceptance criterion is not achievable in principle at this width.

The tension is visible in the fitted quantizers, not just in theory. The per-row search lands
on steps leaving only **0.4–0.5% of coordinates on the box edge**, not the 12.5% a fully
companded source would produce: it is *declining* full companding to stay clear of the faces.
That compromise is why the measured weight-domain margin over NF4 is **+0.23 dB (L10) and
+0.28 dB (L3)** — roughly 57% of the ceiling. The remaining shortfall is construction, not
theory, and is honest to name: the per-row parameter has to serve as both scale and warp
shape so it cannot adapt them independently; the 32-entry LUT is coordinate-wise, whereas the
MSE-optimal reconstruction of an E8 cell is its 8-D centroid, which no product of per-axis
values can express; and both warp and LUT are fitted pooled across rows.

## 21. The metric disagreement returns, and it decides the layer split

| Layer 3 | Act-SNR | KL mean (1e−4) | KL max (1e−4) | CosSim min-token | Top-1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1D-Lloyd-Max | 22.65 | 16.19 | 984 | 0.991030 | 0.9824 |
| 1D-NF4 | 22.62 | 10.50 | 160 | 0.992388 | **0.9863** |
| 1D-NF4-centroid | 22.56 | 12.02 | 266 | 0.991831 | 0.9883 |
| 8D-E8-Uniform | 21.77 | 12.41 | 913 | 0.987324 | 0.9805 |
| **8D-E8-Companded** | **22.96** | **8.95** | 236 | **0.993314** | 0.9766 |

On layer 3 companded E8 has the best Act-SNR, the **lowest logit KL of any scheme** (8.95
vs NF4's 10.50, z = +5.66 paired) and the best worst-token cosine — yet the *worst* top-1
agreement of the non-uniform schemes (0.9766 vs 0.9863). On layer 10 it loses Act-SNR by
0.20 dB and its KL is indistinguishable from NF4's (z = +0.53). In the weight domain it wins
on both layers; the activations reverse the sign on layer 10. That is the fourth time in this
study that a cheaper metric has mis-ranked schemes relative to a more expensive one
(weight SQNR → Act-SNR → logit KL), and the reason the full chain is worth running.

Also worth recording: **`1D-NF4-centroid` is a null result**, 20.38 dB against NF4's 20.38 on
layer 10 and slightly *worse* on layer 3. NF4's quantile levels already sit at the conditional
means to within measurement error, which is a useful fact in itself — and it confirms that
companded E8's win is its cell shape, not its conditional-mean LUT, which is what the control
was there to establish.

## 22. Silicon audit

| Format | loads /8w | int ALU /8w | table entries | lookups /8w | ops /weight | ALU as % of memory time |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1D-NF4 / Lloyd-Max (16-LUT) | 1 | 16 | 16 | 8 | 7.0 | 26.2% |
| 8D-E8 uniform | 1 | 25 | 0 | 0 | 5.1 | 19.2% |
| 8D-E8 radial shell (C1) | 1 | 35 | 16 | **1** | 6.9 | 25.8% |
| 8D-E8 Cartesian 32-LUT (C2) | 1 | 25 | 32 | 8 | 9.1 | 34.2% |

Static instruction counts, not a measured kernel. All formats read identical bytes (28.1 MB,
50 µs at the measured 564 GB/s), so at batch = 1 every dequant cost stays under the memory
time and none of this is decisive. The one structurally interesting column is lookups per 8
weights: Candidate 1 needs *one* table access per block rather than one per weight, which is
the only property here that would survive a port to a different vector unit — and Candidate 1
is the one that buys no accuracy.

**Ascend AIV was not quantified.** Its vector ISA, gather/table primitives and their issue
costs are not something this study can verify, and inventing instruction counts would be
worse than an empty column. What carries over architecturally: uniform and radial-shell E8
need no per-element table and map onto any vector unit with bit-extract + int-to-float + FMA;
NF4 and Cartesian-LUT E8 need a per-element lookup into 16 or 32 entries, which without a
cheap in-register permute becomes a gather or a select chain of depth log₂(entries).

## 23. Recommendation (Part IV)

1. **Reject Candidate 1 outright.** Radial shell scaling is a centroid correction, not
   companding; it buys 0.03 dB of weight SQNR, costs Act-SNR and top-1 agreement, and adds 10
   ALU ops and a table. The mathematics said so in advance and the measurement agrees.
2. **The NF4 recommendation from Part III stands.** Companded E8 is a genuine construction and
   the only thing that ever beat NF4, but it wins on one layer (+0.34 dB, significantly lower
   KL) and loses on the other (−0.20 dB), costs 9.1 ops/weight against 7.0, needs a 32-entry
   per-lane table, and its *ceiling* is +0.405 dB rather than the +0.65 dB that would have
   justified the complexity. A wash across layers is not worth a second kernel.
3. **Do not chase the asymptotic lattice gain at 4 bits/coordinate.** The boundary diagnostic
   is the general lesson: any non-tiling lattice loses 0.25 dB of its packing gain to overload
   in a bounded code at this width, and companding — the very thing that would make the source
   match the density — is what drives mass into those cells. The gain is only fully available
   where the box is not binding, i.e. at higher coordinate width. If 5 bpw were ever on the
   table (Part II, §9), re-run this comparison there; the ceiling rises as the boundary
   fraction falls.
4. **If companded E8 is revisited anyway**, the three named construction limits in §20 are
   worth roughly 0.17 dB between them: decouple the per-row scale from the warp shape, fit the
   warp and LUT per-row-group rather than pooled, and use true 8-D cell centroids instead of a
   coordinate-wise LUT. That would approach +0.405 dB — still short of the criterion.
5. **Keep the boundary diagnostic as a screen.** It takes seconds, needs no model, and would
   have predicted this entire result before any of the model plumbing was written.

## 24. Scope (Part IV)

Adds to §5, §11 and §17. The boundary diagnostic is exact (analytic source, 4 M samples, both
lattices at equal codepoint count). Everything model-side inherits Part III's limits: two
layers of one checkpoint, one tensor type, 512 tokens, logit KL resolved only to a factor of
two against a ~2e−4 nat floor, and a static op count rather than a kernel measurement. The
companded construction is one particular realization; §20 states what it leaves on the table
and bounds the gap at ~0.17 dB, so "companded E8 cannot clear +0.65 dB over NF4 at 4
bits/coordinate" rests on the measured +0.405 dB ceiling, which is construction-independent,
rather than on this implementation's shortfall.

# Part V — Multi-rate sweep b in {3,4,5}: the cross-over, and why b=4 was the worst place to look

Generated by `research/eval_multirate_sweep.py`; tables in
[MULTIRATE_SWEEP.md](MULTIRATE_SWEEP.md), raw numbers in `multirate_report.json`. Same 0.1%
BF16 outliers and same per-row MSE-optimal step search throughout; the 8b-bit register
contract is held and verified lossless at every width.

Two baseline choices are made in the *baseline's* favour, because Parts I–III each reached a
wrong verdict by under-fitting a baseline: **RTN INT-b is asymmetric** (all 2^b codes — the
symmetric convention Part III used throws away 0.56 dB at b=4), and **NF-b is fitted to the
empirical weights**, not to N(0,1). Symmetric-RTN and Gaussian-NF rows are kept at b=4 only,
as diagnostics.

## 25. Tier 1 — the ceiling rises monotonically, and the spec's scaling law is the wrong one

| b | levels | f_boundary = 1−(1−2/2^b)^8 | coords on box edge | unbounded gain | **boxed ceiling** | boundary loss |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | 8 | 90.0% | 25.0% | +0.654 dB | **+0.202 dB** | 0.453 dB |
| 4 | 16 | 65.6% | 12.5% | +0.655 dB | **+0.405 dB** | 0.250 dB |
| 5 | 32 | 40.3% | 6.3% | +0.654 dB | **+0.522 dB** | 0.131 dB |

**Yes, the ceiling rises monotonically toward +0.654 dB** — but even at b=5 the lattice keeps
only 80% of its asymptotic gain. The `unbounded` column (both lattices infinite, granular
error only) returns +0.654 dB at all three widths, which simultaneously validates the boxed
decoder at each one. This formulation replaces Part IV's "hold the source 4 steps inside the
box", which the b=3 box is too small to allow; granular MSE is spatially periodic, so
quantizing with the *infinite* lattices measures the asymptotic value exactly, with no margin
and no edge bias.

**The spec's `f_boundary` is not what the loss tracks.** `f_boundary` falls 90.0 → 65.6 →
40.3% (ratios 1.37, 1.63) while the measured loss falls 0.453 → 0.250 → 0.131 dB (ratios
1.81, 1.91 — essentially halving per bit). What halves exactly is the **edge-coordinate
fraction 2/2^b** (25 → 12.5 → 6.25%). The fraction of cells *touching* a face overstates the
damage, because most such cells are only slightly clipped; what costs distortion is the
fraction of coordinates actually pinned to the edge. Extrapolating: ≈+0.59 dB at b=6,
≈+0.62 dB at b=7.

## 26. The companding strength had to become a free parameter, and what it chose is the result

Part IV §20 named a construction defect: one per-row parameter served as both step size and
warp shape, so the family did not contain uniform E8. At b=3 that is fatal — forced full
companding scored **10.42 dB** on layer 10, 4.5 dB *below* uniform E8. Adding a blend
parameter λ (λ=0 affine, i.e. uniform E8; λ=1 the full quantile warp) makes the family
contain both endpoints, so companded E8 can never lose to uniform E8 by construction. It
lifted b=3 from 10.42 to **15.64 dB**. The b=3 collapse was mine, not the lattice's.

λ is a property of the format (the decoder's LUT depends on it), so it is fitted globally.
Both layers independently chose the same value at every width:

| b | λ chosen | coords on box edge | what it means |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | **0.25** | 8.6–9.4% | light companding; the box is starting to bind |
| 4 | **0.00** | 2.7% | full companding *rejected* — pure uniform E8 plus a centroid LUT |
| 5 | **1.00** | 0.1% | full companding, and the boundary is cheap enough to afford it |

That is the Part IV tension quantified: companding only pays once the box stops binding.

## 27. The two gains scale in opposite directions, and they cross between b=3 and b=4

Weight-SQNR gain over the *same-width* uniform scalar baseline (RTN INT-b), layer 3 / layer 10:

| b | lattice gain (E8-Uniform − RTN) | companding gain (NF-b − RTN) | larger |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | **+0.960 / +0.950** | +0.569 / +0.545 | **lattice** |
| 4 | +0.706 / +0.712 | **+0.848 / +0.902** | companding |
| 5 | +0.648 / +0.617 | **+1.466 / +1.585** | **companding** |

**The lattice gain is flat-to-declining (0.96 → 0.65) while 1-D companding gain nearly
triples (0.56 → 1.53). They cross between b=3 and b=4.** This *inverts* the working
hypothesis, which expected the lattice to be destroyed at b=3 and to win at b=5.

The reason the boundary law does not govern this column: on a real *peaked* weight source,
uniform E8's box barely binds (2.7% of coordinates on the edge at b=4), so it collects close
to its full packing gain regardless of b — plus extra, because E8's half-integer coset gives
each coordinate a half-step alphabet, which is worth more when steps are coarse. The
boundary-fraction law governs the *companded* comparison, where the source is deliberately
flattened onto the box. That is exactly why λ=1 is chosen only at b=5.

Meanwhile companding gain grows with b because these weights are heavy-tailed (kurtosis 4.25,
max/rms 52.9): a uniform codebook keeps spending range on the tail, and the more levels
available, the more a density-matched codebook can exploit.

## 28. The ceiling is predictive exactly where it applies

| b | λ | E8-Companded W-SQNR margin vs NF-b (L3 / L10) | boxed ceiling | frac of ceiling |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | 0.25 | +0.786 / +0.832 | +0.202 | 3.90 / 4.13 |
| 4 | 0.00 | +0.319 / +0.264 | +0.405 | 0.79 / 0.65 |
| 5 | **1.00** | **+0.519 / +0.584** | **+0.522** | **0.99 / 1.12** |

At b=5, where the scheme actually is fully companded, the measured margin matches the
independently-measured ceiling to within 12% on both layers — a clean confirmation that the
Tier-1 diagnostic predicts the real tensor. At b=3 and b=4 the ratio is meaningless and is
reported only for completeness: the ceiling bounds the packing component of a *box-filling*
scheme, and at λ<1 the scheme is not box-filling, so it draws on the interior (nearer
+0.654 dB) and forfeits part of the density matching instead.

## 29. Answers to the four questions

**Does the ceiling rise monotonically toward +0.654 dB?** Yes — +0.202, +0.405, +0.522 —
approaching but never reaching it; 80% at b=5.

**At b=5, does companded E8 definitively beat NF5 on both layers, in Act-SNR and logit KL?**
Act-SNR: **yes, decisively** — **+2.714 dB** (layer 3) and **+1.218 dB** (layer 10), and it
also takes best W-SQNR, best mean KL, best worst-token cosine and best top-1 on both layers.
Logit KL by the z>3 bar: **layer 3 yes (z = +4.42), layer 10 no (z = +1.94)**, so not
"definitive" as specified, though mean KL is lower on both (2.58 vs 2.80; 4.92 vs 4.94).

**At b=3, does boundary loss destroy the lattice and make NF3 strictly superior?** **No — the
opposite.** E8-Companded-3 beats NF3 on every metric on both layers (Act-SNR +0.927/+0.748,
W-SQNR +0.786/+0.832, KL z = +4.42/+5.48, both clearing z>3), and even *uniform* E8-3 beats
NF3 on SNR. The premise fails because the box only binds when the source is companded, and at
b=3 the optimizer declines most of the companding (λ=0.25) precisely to avoid that.

**Pareto-optimal format per rate** (agreeing on Act-SNR, logit KL and worst-token cosine,
both layers):

| rate | Pareto-optimal | margin over the best 1-D scalar |
|---|---|---|
| **3.015 bpw** | **E8-Companded-3** (λ=0.25) | +0.75 to +0.93 dB Act-SNR, KL z>3 on both layers |
| **4.013 bpw** | **NF4** — a tie, so take the simpler kernel | E8-Companded-4 is +0.04/−0.14 dB; nothing to buy |
| **5.014 bpw** | **E8-Companded-5** (λ=1.0) | +1.22 to +2.71 dB Act-SNR, best on every metric |

## 30. b=4 is the worst width at which to judge the lattice

The single most useful thing in this sweep: the lattice wins at b=3 by one mechanism (coset
resolution on a peaked source), wins at b=5 by another (companding plus packing, boundary
now cheap), and **ties at b=4, caught between the two** — which is why λ=0 there, and why
Parts III and IV, which only ever looked at b=4, concluded against it. The advantage is
non-monotone in b, and b=4 sits in its trough.

A methodological note on the KL criterion, too: z>3 is met at b=3 on both layers but not at
b=5 on layer 10, even though b=5 has a far larger Act-SNR margin. That is the Part III floor
limitation again — at b=3 the KLs (11.3 vs 14.1e−4) sit well above the 1.87e−4 floor and
resolve easily; at b=5 (2.58 vs 2.80e−4) almost all of the signal *is* floor. The z>3 bar
therefore measures how much headroom the metric has as much as how good the scheme is, and it
gets harder as the scheme gets better.

## 31. Recommendation (Part V) — conditional on the rate the engine ships

1. **At 4 bpw, nothing changes: ship NF4.** Part III's recommendation survives its own
   re-examination at the width it was made for. E8-Companded-4 is a tie (+0.04 dB layer 10,
   −0.14 dB layer 3) and costs a 32-entry per-lane table plus the E8 unpack.
2. **At 5 bpw, ship E8-Companded-5.** +1.2 to +2.7 dB Act-SNR over NF5, best on every metric
   on both layers, at 5.014 bpw against NF5's 5.012. This is the configuration Part IV said to
   re-test at higher width, and it wins. It is also the rate Part II §9 identified as the only
   one reaching a 0.999 cosine floor, so if that requirement is real, this is the format.
3. **At 3 bpw, ship E8-Companded-3.** It beats NF3 on all three metrics on both layers with
   KL significance, which is the clearest lattice win anywhere in this study.
4. **Never evaluate a lattice format at a single bitwidth.** The advantage is non-monotone,
   with a trough at exactly the width everyone tests.
5. **Fit companding strength; do not assume it.** λ is one global scalar, free to store, and
   choosing it badly cost 4.5 dB at b=3. Its fitted value (0.25 / 0.00 / 1.00) is also the
   most compact summary of the whole rate-distortion trade-off in this study.

## 32. Scope (Part V)

Adds to §5, §11, §17 and §24. Tier 1 is exact (analytic source, 10^7 vectors per width, both
lattices at equal codepoint count, float64 accumulation). Tier 2 inherits every Part III
limit: two layers of one checkpoint, one tensor type, 512 tokens, logit KL resolved only to a
factor of two against a ~2e−4 nat floor — which §30 shows now binds the b=5 comparison — and a
static op count rather than a kernel measurement. λ is fitted on the same weights it is
evaluated on, as are every codebook and warp here, so all rows share that optimism equally;
none of them is validated on held-out tensors. As a cross-check that this harness agrees with
the earlier ones, the b=4 rows reproduce Parts III and IV exactly for every scheme that is
literally the same quantizer: NF4 (20.38 dB / 19.69 dB weight SQNR on layer 10),
RTN-INT4-sym (18.74 / 18.42) and E8-Uniform-4 (19.86 / 19.50). E8-Companded-4 is the one row
that legitimately differs (20.41 vs Part IV's 20.18), because lambda is now fitted rather than
pinned at 1.
